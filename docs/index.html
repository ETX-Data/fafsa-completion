    How these numbers are calculated
    • Completion rate = FAFSAs completed ÷ graduates, added up across districts. District-years where TEA suppressed the completion count are left out of both.
    • “No FAFSA” = graduates − completions.
    • “Districts below 50%” counts districts with a rate under 50% and at least one completion. The district table also leaves out districts reporting zero completions, which are usually reporting gaps.
    • “Change” is the difference in completion rate, in percentage points, between 2022–23 and the selected school year.
    • “Similar districts” are those with the same TEA community type (all charters, for a charter district).
    • The trend line is a simple least-squares fit of completion rate on % economically disadvantaged across the districts in the table. % economically disadvantaged comes from each district’s latest Texas Academic Performance Report.
    • Federal Student Aid (FSA) counts are FAFSAs completed by first-time applicants 19 or younger who reported a public high school, summed to the district through the federal–TEA district ID match in the NCES directory. FSA publishes no graduate counts, so its counts aren’t turned into rates; its “est. rate” bins are FSA’s own estimates. A district is flagged when TEA’s 2024–25 completions are under half of FSA’s count for the same class (30+ in FSA).
    • A school district counts as serving a Texas House or Senate district when their overlap is at least 5% of either one’s land area, or it is the school district’s largest overlap. One school district can serve several legislative districts. Charter districts have no boundaries and are not counted.
